Theory: constrained Monte Carlo

The sampler behind run_cmc (Asselin et al., Phys. Rev. B 82, 054415 (2010); weighted compensation after Toga et al., arXiv:1606.00333; the hybrid rule after Ellis et al., arXiv:1706.05188). The standing contract, with every convention, door, and gate, is the decision record docs/specs/constrained-mc.md (D1–D5 below); usage is in the guide.

The constrained ensemble (D1)

Each cell atom $a$ carries a signed weight $w_a$. The constrained sites are $\mathcal C = \{s : s \text{ active},\ w_{a(s)} \neq 0\}$ and the constrained magnetization is

\[\mathbf M = \sum_{s\in\mathcal C} w_{a(s)}\,\mathbf e_s,\qquad M_\parallel = \mathbf M\cdot\hat n,\qquad \mathbf M_\perp = \mathbf M - M_\parallel\,\hat n .\]

The sampled measure, defined exactly at finite $N$, is the canonical one restricted to $\hat{\mathbf M} = \hat n$ by the delta function on the sphere,

\[\pi(d\xi) \propto \prod_s d\Omega_s\; e^{-\beta E(\xi)}\; \delta_{S^2}\bigl(\hat{\mathbf M}(\xi), \hat n\bigr),\qquad \delta_{S^2}(\hat{\mathbf M}, \hat n) = |\mathbf M|^2\,\delta^2(\mathbf M_\perp)\, \Theta(M_\parallel).\]

The factor $|\mathbf M|^2 = M_\parallel^2$ is the Jacobian between the transverse components of $\mathbf M$ and the solid angle of its direction. With it, the normalization of $\pi$ is the canonical probability density $P(\hat n)$ of $\hat{\mathbf M}$ per solid angle, and

\[F(\hat n) = -k_BT \ln P(\hat n) + \text{const}\]

is the free energy of the magnetization direction of this finite system (Asselin's $F$). Dropping the factor gives another ensemble whose free energy differs by $O(k_BT/N)$ per spin; that measure-dependent part is why $F$ is a finite-size quantity to be extrapolated in $N$. Inactive sites are excluded from $\mathbf M$ and from every observable, and stay frozen.

The weighted pair move (D2)

A move picks a source $i\in\mathcal C$ and a partner $j \in \mathcal C \setminus \{i\}$, rotates $\mathbf e_i \to \mathbf e_i'$ by a symmetric Rodrigues proposal (uniform axis, angle step · randn, no flips), and lets $\mathbf e_j$ restore $\mathbf M_\perp$:

\[\mathbf e_{j\perp}' = \mathbf e_{j\perp} - \frac{w_i}{w_j}\bigl(\mathbf e_{i\perp}' - \mathbf e_{i\perp}\bigr),\qquad e_{j\parallel}' = \sigma_j\sqrt{1 - |\mathbf e_{j\perp}'|^2},\qquad \sigma_j = \operatorname{sign} e_{j\parallel} .\]

It is a null move — rejected, the state unchanged — when $|\mathbf e_{j\perp}'| \ge 1$ (the compensating spin would leave the sphere) or when $M_\parallel' \le 0$ (the magnetization would cross into the other hemisphere). Otherwise it is accepted with probability $\min(1, e^{\ell})$,

\[\ell = -\beta\,\Delta E + 2\ln\frac{M_\parallel'}{M_\parallel} + \ln|e_{j\parallel}| - \ln|e_{j\parallel}'| ,\]

where $\Delta E$ is the exact joint energy change of the two spins, computed by two sequential single-site differences (the second one against the tabulated harmonics with $\mathbf e_i'$ already in place), so it is exact for any body order even when $i$ and $j$ share a cluster.

Derivation. Hold every spin except $i$ and $j$ fixed. The constraint fixes $w_j\mathbf e_{j\perp} = \mathbf C - w_i\mathbf e_{i\perp}$ for a constant $\mathbf C$, so $\mathbf e_i$ is free on its sphere and $\mathbf e_j$ is determined up to the hemisphere $\sigma_j$. Integrating $\pi$ over $\mathbf e_j$ uses the projection of the sphere onto the plane $\perp \hat n$, $d\Omega_j = d^2 e_{j\perp}/|e_{j\parallel}|$ (on each hemisphere), and $\delta^2(w_j\mathbf e_{j\perp} - \dots) = \delta^2(\mathbf e_{j\perp} - \dots)/w_j^2$. The density of the pair state on this fiber, with respect to $d\Omega_i$, is therefore

\[\rho(\mathbf e_i) \propto e^{-\beta E}\;\frac{M_\parallel^2}{w_j^2\,|e_{j\parallel}|}.\]

The rotation of $\mathbf e_i$ is symmetric and the compensation is a deterministic function of $\mathbf e_i'$ that keeps $\sigma_j$, so the reverse move (same ordered pair) has the same proposal density, and the partner is drawn with a state-independent probability. The Metropolis ratio is the ratio of fiber densities: $w_j^2$ cancels and $\ell$ above follows. Null moves are exactly the proposals that land where $\rho = 0$.

Null moves must be counted. A null move is an ordinary rejected attempt: the chain stays put and the attempt counts. Re-drawing a new proposal until one is geometrically valid instead turns the kernel into $K(x\to y)/(1 - r(x))$, with $r(x)$ the null-move probability from $x$; its stationary distribution is $\pi(x)\,(1 - r(x))$, not $\pi$ — a bias the package's investigation measured at 8–13σ on three spins. Counting them also means the acceptance that drives the thermalization-only step adaptation includes the null moves, and acceptance_metropolis of a constrained run is the accepted fraction of all attempts.

The partner rule. The partner is uniform over $\mathcal C\setminus\{i\}$. Restricting it to a partner of the same weight would conserve the transverse sum of each weight class separately and break ergodicity.

Signed weights — the gauge map. With $\tilde{\mathbf e}_s = \operatorname{sign}(w_s)\,\mathbf e_s$, $\mathbf M = \sum_s |w_s|\,\tilde{\mathbf e}_s$: the signed problem maps exactly onto the $|w|$ problem. The compensation (the ratio $w_i/w_j$ carries the relative sign), the hemisphere $\sigma_j$, the Jacobian ($|e_{j\parallel}|$ is unchanged by a sign flip), and the symmetry of the proposal all commute with the map, so negative weights need no special case: an antiparallel sublattice with a negative weight is the $|w|$ problem in a relabeled frame.

The sweep and the hybrid rule (D3)

One sweep visits the active sites in site order: a pair attempt with source $s$ for $s \in \mathcal C$, a plain single-spin Metropolis attempt (flip proposal included) for an active site with $w = 0$. A weight-0 site does not enter $\mathbf M$, so moving it leaves the constraint intact, and its single-site kernel preserves $\pi$ (its conditional density is just $e^{-\beta E}$): the hybrid rule — part of the system constrained, the rest free. A fixed-order composition of $\pi$-preserving kernels preserves $\pi$.

Why the sweep is serial. The single-spin sweeps update a colour class in parallel because kernels of sites that share no cluster commute. Pair moves never do: every acceptance reads the global $M_\parallel$, which every accepted pair changes, and the partner can be any site of $\mathcal C$. So the CMC sweep runs on one task (sweep_tasks is refused); the parallelism is across directions (lanes). Every attempt draws only from its source site's own random stream, in a fixed order (partner, rotation axis, angle, and the accept uniform only when needed), so a run is bit-reproducible for a fixed seed and independent of the thread count.

The torque identity (D5)

The torque of a configuration is $\mathbf T = \sum_{s\,\text{active}} \mathbf G_s\times\mathbf e_s = -\sum_s \mathbf e_s\times\partial E/\partial\mathbf e_s$, with sign fixed by a rigid rotation of every spin by $\alpha$ about the unit axis $\mathbf w$: $\mathbf T\cdot\mathbf w = -dE/d\alpha$. Every term counts with unit weight — the weights shape the constraint, not the energy.

Rotate every spin rigidly by $R_\alpha$ (angle $\alpha$ about $\mathbf w$). The product measure is invariant and $\hat{\mathbf M}$ rotates with the spins, so substituting $\xi \to R_\alpha\xi$ gives $P(R_\alpha\hat n)/P(\hat n) = \langle e^{-\beta[E(R_\alpha\xi) - E(\xi)]}\rangle_{\hat n}$ and, differentiating at $\alpha = 0$, $\frac{d}{d\alpha}F(R_\alpha\hat n) = \langle dE/d\alpha\rangle = -\langle\mathbf T\rangle\cdot\mathbf w$. With $dR_\alpha\hat n/d\alpha = \mathbf w\times\hat n$, for every axis $\mathbf w$,

\[\langle\mathbf T\rangle = -\hat n\times\frac{\partial F}{\partial\hat n},\]

exact at finite $N$ and independent of the weights (they enter only through which $\hat{\mathbf M}$ is constrained). In the polar frame $\hat e_\theta = \partial\hat n/\partial\theta$, $\hat e_\varphi = (-\sin\varphi, \cos\varphi, 0)$:

\[\langle\mathbf T\rangle\cdot\hat e_\varphi = -\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta},\qquad \langle\mathbf T\rangle\cdot\hat e_\theta = \frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial F}{\partial\varphi},\qquad \langle\mathbf T\rangle\cdot\hat n = 0 .\]

The observables report per cell of H ($\mathbf T/n_\text{cells}$, the gradient of $F/n_\text{cells}$, with $n_\text{cells} = n_\text{sites}/n_\text{cell atoms}$ counting the cells of H itself — the reduced cell after reduce_cell): :torque in Cartesian components and :torque_frame as the three projections above, $(\hat e_\varphi, \hat e_\theta, \hat n)\cdot\mathbf T$ (the $\varphi = 0$ frame at the poles). The projection is binned as its own observable because its error bar needs the covariance of the Cartesian components, which per-component errors do not carry. A field contributes the analytic Zeeman torque $\mu_B(\mathbf M_\mu\times\mathbf B)$, $\mathbf M_\mu = \sum m_{a(s)}\mathbf e_s$ (a moment is turned toward $+\mathbf B$).

Partial torques. For a disjoint split of the fitted terms, the torques of the term-subset Hamiltonians sum to the total configuration by configuration (plus the Zeeman part). Each partial torque alone, $\langle\mathbf T_c\rangle$, equals $-\hat n\times\partial F_c/\partial\hat n$ for a well-defined share $F_c$ only to first order in the anisotropy: beyond it, the constrained average of one part picks up covariance terms with the others, so the single-ion / two-ion split of $K$ is unique only in the $O(K)$ limit.

Linear response. To first order in a single-ion term $\sum_s v(\mathbf e_s)$ the anisotropy is the isotropic average over the fluctuations of each spin about $\hat{\mathbf M}$; for $v \propto P_l$ this is $K_\text{eff}/K = \langle P_l(\mathbf e\cdot\hat{\mathbf M})\rangle_\text{iso}$, the exact $O(K)$ reference; its low-temperature asymptote is the classical Callen–Callen law $\sim m^{l(l+1)/2}$, a useful guide but not an identity. The package checks its sampler against this identity, against direct histograms of $\hat{\mathbf M}$ from unconstrained runs, and against rotated-iid references for three- and four-body models (the "Manual validation" section of the decision record).
